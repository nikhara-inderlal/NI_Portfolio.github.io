Leading Analytics Delivery at a National Youth Organisation

Leading a six-person analytics team through end-to-end delivery of five Power BI dashboards, where the delivery structure mattered as much as the reporting.

SectorNational Youth Organisation
RoleProject Lead / Analytics Consultant
EngagementSix-person analytics team, five dashboards
ToolsPower BI, Amazon Redshift, Power Query, Monday.com, Excel

Context

The client is a national youth-development organisation. The organisation needed a refreshed reporting suite supporting several levels of management, from head office through to the people running local programmes. Existing reporting was inconsistent and depended on heavy manual effort.

The delivery constraint was as significant as the analytical one: five dashboards, six analysts, multiple stakeholder groups, and dashboards built by different people that still had to look and behave like one product.

Business problem

Different management levels were reporting different totals for the same programmes, and the manual rollup was slow. Underneath that sat a coordination problem. Without shared requirements, a common data model and agreed design conventions, six analysts building in parallel would produce five dashboards that each answered their own question in their own visual language.

Approach

I led the team through end-to-end delivery of the five dashboards, coordinating work allocation, requirements, blockers, design reviews and progress. I introduced Monday.com for task tracking and promoted an iterative Agile delivery approach with regular team syncs, while contributing directly to the Redshift and Power Query modelling and maintaining documentation and UX consistency through to stakeholder sign-off.

Alongside the dashboards I built the delivery controls: a requirements and checklist framework to track blockers and completion, and transformation and calculation documentation maintained in Excel with controlled inputs.

Modelling & preparation

Connected Power BI to Amazon Redshift and used Power Query to clean, reshape and enhance source data, collaborating on the data model including relationships and calculated columns. Page layouts were tailored to each management level while sharing the same underlying metrics, so numbers reconcile across views rather than diverging by audience.

Validation

Ran design reviews across the dashboards built by different analysts to keep calculation logic and visual conventions consistent, and maintained the transformation and calculation documentation as the single reference when two dashboards appeared to disagree.

Stakeholder delivery & handover

Facilitated team meetings, provided peer feedback and design guidance, and took the reporting suite through final stakeholder review and sign-off. The documentation framework went with it, so the work did not depend on the analysts who built it.

Outcomes

Five dashboards delivered as one coherent suite reporting on consistent numbers across management levels, with the documentation and requirements framework handed over alongside them.

The part I would draw attention to is that the delivery structure was itself a deliverable. A six-person team building in parallel does not converge on its own, and the tracking, checklists, documentation standard and design reviews are what made five separate builds read as one product.

Reflection

Project coordination mattered as much as the BI build. Keeping a single tracker, an agreed cadence and named owners stopped the work from sprawling and made the handover clean. Leading analysts rather than just doing the analysis was the genuinely new skill here, and the lesson was that design consistency has to be enforced continuously rather than reviewed at the end.
