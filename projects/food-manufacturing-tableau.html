Manufacturing Cost Analytics & Forecasting

Giving site managers a way to find where cost variance was occurring and investigate what was driving it, rather than presenting them with the variance and leaving them there.

SectorFood Manufacturing / FMCG Client, part of one of Australia and New Zealand’s largest food manufacturing groups
RoleReporting Analyst / Tableau Developer
EngagementManufacturing and distribution operations, 12 business periods
ToolsTableau, Alteryx, Excel

Context

The client is a food manufacturing and FMCG business, part of one of the largest food manufacturing groups in Australia and New Zealand. Operational leaders across manufacturing and distribution sites needed to monitor and forecast expenses across cost categories including Labour, Utilities, Occupancy, Repairs and Maintenance and Depreciation.

Business problem

Knowing that a site is over forecast is not actionable on its own. The useful question is which cost category, and then which specific expense, is driving it. Without a path from the headline number down to the underlying detail, variance reviews are spent establishing what happened rather than discussing why, and there is no consistent basis for comparing efficiency between sites of different sizes.

Approach

I designed and delivered the original end-to-end Tableau structure for monitoring and forecasting expenses across manufacturing and distribution. The organising principle was a single navigable path: site performance, then EBIT mapping, then cost category, then individual expense detail, so a manager can start at the headline and arrive at the cause without leaving the report.

Separate MTD and YTD Actual versus Forecast views carry that hierarchy, supported by dynamic tooltips and conditional colour changes on the Actual bars. I used bullet-chart style comparison with Forecast as the reference marker, which shows overspend and underspend compactly without needing separate variance columns.

Modelling & preparation

Used Alteryx for data cleaning, type standardisation and preparation before Tableau modelling. Integrated 3+9 multi-version forecast logic across 12 business periods, so scenario-based projections can be compared against actual expenditure at multiple levels of detail.

I also personally introduced $/tonne cost-efficiency analysis for manufacturing and distribution, including MTD and running-total Actual versus Forecast trends. Normalising cost by volume is what makes site comparison fair, since a larger site spending more is not the same as a less efficient one.

Validation

Checked the prepared cost and forecast data through the Alteryx preparation stage and documented all measures so the calculation behind each figure was traceable, which matters when a site manager challenges a variance that reflects on their operation.

Stakeholder delivery & handover

Presented the completed solution and achieved stakeholder sign-off, and delivered a fully recorded walkthrough with usage guidance and analytical insights so the solution remained maintainable and usable independently after handover.

Outcomes

Site managers can move from site performance through EBIT to cost category to individual expense in one place, with MTD and YTD actual versus forecast, $/tonne efficiency and forecast-scenario comparison available at each level.

The change is in what the reporting supports. Rather than distributing variance figures and leaving interpretation to each site, it gives managers a route to the driver behind the number, which is the difference between a report that reports and one that gets used to investigate.

Reflection

Restraint on KPI count was the call that mattered most. The initial brief asked for a long list of metrics, and agreeing on a smaller, sharper set made the drill path legible. The $/tonne analysis is the piece I am most satisfied with, because it was not asked for and it is what made cross-site comparison defensible.
