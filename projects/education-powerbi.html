Education Analytics: Student Progress & Effort Reporting

Comparing students on raw attainment hides the more interesting question — how much progress did each one make from their own starting point? This case is about designing metrics that answer that question instead of the easier one.

The problem

Education reporting tends to default to raw attainment, which advantages whoever started higher. The teaching team wanted reporting that surfaced progress and effort fairly — so the conversation could move from rankings to trajectories.

What I built

Baseline-normalised metrics in Power BI: each student's progress measured against their own starting point, with longitudinal views that read at both the individual and the cohort level. DAX did most of the heavy lifting on the metric side.

Validation

The validation here is interpretive as much as numerical — checking that a high progress signal corresponded to genuine improvement rather than a measurement artefact, and that the metric definitions held up to scrutiny from teachers who knew the underlying students.

What changed

Reporting that supported a fairer conversation about progress. As much a metric-design exercise as a Power BI build, and a reminder that the hardest part is often what you choose to measure.
