Education Analytics: Student Progress & Effort Reporting

Power BI reporting for a private school using a baseline-aware effort score, with walkthroughs for teachers and the principal.

SectorEducation
RoleData Analyst
EngagementProject engagement
ToolsPower BI, Power Query, DAX, Semantic model

Context

A private school wanted to track student progress and effort over time. The existing simple averaging approach unfairly penalised students who had always performed at the top of the range, and unfairly rewarded students whose baseline was low. The principal wanted a fairer metric.

Business problem

A single average mark hides effort. Students consistently performing at the top of the range looked the same year-on-year even when they were extending themselves, and students with lower baselines could appear weak even when their own trajectory was strong.

Approach

Designed a baseline-aware effort score in DAX that compared each student's current term to their own historical performance, not to a year-group mean. Built a Power BI reporting page per year group plus a per-student deep-dive, and walked teachers and the principal through it for sign-off.

Modelling & preparation

dim_student, dim_year_group, dim_subject, dim_term, fact_assessment. DAX measures for current-term mean, baseline mean (excluding current term), effort score (delta vs baseline normalised by standard deviation), and trend.

Validation

Walked the principal through a sample of high-baseline and low-baseline students to confirm the metric matched what teachers actually felt was going on. Adjusted normalisation parameters until the score matched professional judgement.

Stakeholder delivery & handover

Walkthroughs with teachers and the principal, plus a short written explanation of how the effort score was calculated and its limitations, so the metric could be defended to parents and used consistently across staff.

Outcomes

Teachers now have an objective effort metric that doesn't penalise consistently strong students. Conversations with parents are easier because the score is explainable and defensible. The principal reviews trends across year groups without sifting through spreadsheets.

Reflection

Designing a metric is a stakeholder conversation, not a maths exercise. The normalisation parameters had to match what teachers already sensed about their students, otherwise the metric would have been dismissed regardless of how mathematically tidy it was.
