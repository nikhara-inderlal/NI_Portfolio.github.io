Student Progress & Effort Analytics

Twenty years of assessment history, and a measurement problem: how do you recognise genuine improvement when the difficulty of the work rises every year?

SectorIndependent Education Client
RoleAnalytics Consultant / Power BI Developer
Engagement20+ years of assessment history, 2005 to 2025
ToolsPower BI, Power Query, DAX, Mathematical modelling

Context

The client is an independent school holding more than twenty years of assessment history, from 2005 to 2025. The school wanted to track student progress and effort over time, but the existing approach relied on simple averaging, which unfairly penalised students who had always performed at the top of the range and unfairly rewarded students whose baseline was low. The principal wanted a fairer metric.

Business problem

A single average mark hides effort, and the obvious alternatives are worse. Comparing a student against their year-group mean measures the cohort, not the student. Comparing raw marks across years ignores that year-level difficulty rises, so a student holding a steady mark while the work gets harder is actually improving and looks static.

This matters because the metric was going to be used in conversations with teachers and parents. A measure that cannot be explained, or that visibly rewards the wrong students, gets dismissed regardless of how defensible it is mathematically.

Approach

I treated this as a metric-design problem rather than a reporting problem. I applied mathematical and statistical reasoning to develop a custom Effort Score comparing current performance against each student's own historical baseline, adjusted for year-level difficulty, then developed a linear weighting model to improve comparability between grades of differing difficulty.

The design decision that mattered most was making the methodology recognise both genuine improvement and sustained high performance. A model that only rewards change punishes the student who was already excellent and stayed there.

Modelling & preparation

Restructured multi-year, multi-subject assessment data into a clean Student by Subject by Term analytical model using Power Query merges, grouping, transformations and calculated logic. Built an interactive single-student Power BI report carrying year level, subject count, average effort and overall mark change, plus a detailed subject matrix of mark, historical average, weighted difference, percentage change and effort.

Longitudinal views across years and semesters let teachers analyse performance and effort over time rather than relying on peer comparison at a single point.

Validation

The test of a metric like this is not whether it computes. It is whether the students it flags are the students teachers already sense are extending themselves. I presented the analytical methodology alongside the reporting solution so the weighting logic and its limitations were visible and open to challenge, rather than arriving as a black-box score.

Stakeholder delivery & handover

Presented the analytical methodology and the completed reporting solution through stakeholder review and sign-off, so the approach was understood and accepted by the people who would have to defend it in conversations with staff and parents.

Outcomes

The school gained a progress measure that compares each student against their own trajectory rather than against their peers, and that recognises sustained high performance as well as improvement. Because the methodology was explained rather than delivered as a number, it can be defended in the conversations it was built for.

Reflection

Designing a metric is a stakeholder conversation, not a maths exercise. The mathematics decides whether the measure is sound; the conversation decides whether anyone uses it. This is the project I would point to as evidence that a statistics background is worth something beyond the model itself, because the hard part was defining fairness precisely enough to compute it.
