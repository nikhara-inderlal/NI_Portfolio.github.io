Online Retail Client Engagement: Database Integrity & Order Validation

A client-led database engineering engagement, moving order and fulfilment data quality from reactive cleansing to enforcement at source.

SectorOnline retail
RoleSQL Developer / PostgreSQL / PL-pgSQL
EngagementRequirements gathering through to solution presentation and sign-off
ToolsPostgreSQL, PL/pgSQL, DDL, Triggers, Audit logging, DBeaver

Context

Client-led technical engagement delivered against a simulated online retail environment. The underlying environment was simulated, while the requirements gathering, solution development, presentation and stakeholder delivery process followed a real client engagement workflow.

The problem was a familiar one in order systems. Orders change state in unexpected ways, fulfilment events arrive out of order, and the order header quietly disagrees with the sum of its line items.

Business problem

The usual response to this class of problem is downstream cleansing, which treats the symptom indefinitely and never removes the cause. Bad rows still enter the database, and everything built on top of them inherits the error.

The requirement I took from the client was to identify where integrity actually breaks across orders, line items, financial values and fulfilment status, and to decide whether those problems should be detected after the fact or prevented at the point of entry.

Approach

Analysed the PostgreSQL schema, table relationships, dependencies and order-processing logic to identify data-quality and integrity risks. Defined and implemented business rules, validation checks and database constraints covering minimum required fields, dependent attributes and valid status progression.

Developed PL/pgSQL functions to keep fulfilment and delivery status aligned with the true progression of each order, then built event-driven triggers and alert logic to detect and prevent invalid transactions at source, shifting the solution from reactive cleansing toward preventative data-quality enforcement.

Claude AI was used as a technical support tool to accelerate SQL logic development and troubleshooting. Final logic was reviewed, tested and validated before being treated as the project output.

Modelling & preparation

Created a central audit-log table capturing trigger events, alert type, severity and descriptions, giving full traceability of database-level validation activity. Naming each rule and assigning it a severity turned raw trigger output into a prioritised feed rather than undifferentiated noise.

Validation

Tested each business rule against transactions designed to violate it, confirming the constraint or trigger fired with the correct rule and severity recorded in the audit log, and that valid status progressions passed through unaffected.

Stakeholder delivery & handover

Presented a structured technical walkthrough to the client covering the schema analysis, identified issues, remediation, business rules, triggers, alerts and audit design, and secured sign-off on the approach.

Outcomes

Invalid transactions are prevented at source rather than corrected downstream, and the audit log provides a traceable record of validation activity showing how often each rule fires and why.

Reflection

The decision that mattered was where to place the logic. Detection in the reporting layer is quicker to build but leaves the bad data in place for everything downstream to inherit. Pushing enforcement into constraints and triggers costs more up front and removes the problem, which is the same reasoning I apply to handling data quality in the curation layer rather than patching it at report level.
