Enterprise Healthcare Analytics

A year inside a governed Microsoft Fabric estate serving 200+ sites across Australia and New Zealand, working across four business domains from SME requirements through modelling, validation and production release.

ClientLarge Australasian Healthcare Organisation, 200+ sites across AU and NZ
RoleAnalytics Engineer / Power BI Consultant
EngagementSep 2025 to Present
ToolsFabric (Lakehouse/Warehouse, Direct Lake), Dataflow Gen2, Power BI, DAX, T-SQL, Spark, Power Apps, Power Automate, SharePoint Lists, D365, PayGlobal, Excel, Azure DevOps, Confluence

Context

The client is a large Australasian healthcare organisation supporting more than 200 sites across Australia and New Zealand. Reporting was fragmented across teams and domains, with inconsistent metric definitions, limited shared modelling and manual validation work, and gaps between source systems, curated reporting tables and final Power BI outputs. Source data spans Microsoft Dynamics 365, PayGlobal and multiple practice-management-system structures. Without a common semantic layer it was difficult to reconcile KPIs or to give leadership a defensible audit path from source to published figure.

Business problem

Stakeholders needed a single, governed reporting layer that could support KPI and variance reporting, point-in-time snapshots and exception monitoring, while also reducing remediation time when source data had gaps. The previous setup did not expose missing reference mappings, so downstream transaction reporting silently lost rows.

The consequences were practical rather than theoretical. Australia and New Zealand ran separate reporting with duplicated logic. Patient counts disagreed between reports because legacy logic mixed appointment-based activity counts with patient-dimension cohort counts. Clinical exception review depended on analysts rebuilding reports whenever a business threshold changed. Finance filled the gaps with manual lookups.

My role and ownership

I own delivery on this engagement from requirements through to production release. That means I lead the SME sessions with Finance, Marketing and Clinical Assurance stakeholders myself, translate what they describe into analytical requirements, design and build the solution, unit test and reconcile it, produce the technical and business documentation, run the walkthrough, incorporate feedback, obtain sign-off and deploy through controlled TEST to PROD release.

The build work is a mix of independent and collaborative. I work across a governed 70-table Gold-layer Direct Lake model built to strict star-schema standards; ten of those Gold tables I built independently, several of them end to end from Bronze through Gold. I have also improved upstream Bronze and Silver transformations, created the dimensional relationships and authored more than 400 report-specific measures.

Approach

Rebuilt and standardised reporting across the four domains on a shared Direct Lake semantic model, with curated fact and dimension structures for consistency and scale. The work spans the curation layer (T-SQL and Spark notebooks in Fabric), the semantic model layer (reusable governed measures), the report layer (Power BI and Excel), and a workflow layer where SharePoint Lists, Power Apps and Power Automate turn a report into something users can act inside. Previously fragmented reporting was consolidated into shared Australia and New Zealand solutions with row-level security and reusable governed measures, improving consistency across regions and removing duplicated report logic.

The analytics estate

Created and enriched reporting tables using T-SQL and Spark notebooks in Fabric Lakehouse and Warehouse, built to strict star-schema standards. Built reusable governed base measures so the same KPI means the same thing across reports, rather than being redefined report by report.

Metric integrity was part of the work rather than an afterthought. I standardised patient-count definitions across reports by correcting legacy logic that mixed appointment-based activity counts with patient-dimension cohort counts. That is a small technical change with a disproportionate effect: it is the difference between two reports that disagree and two reports the business can trust.

Clinical Assurance: self-service thresholds and exception workflow

Clinical exception reporting required users to identify, investigate, categorise and comment on exceptions, and every change to a business threshold meant going back to an analyst for a report change. The reporting was creating analyst dependency rather than removing it.

I built two self-service Clinical Assurance dashboards spanning more than twelve report pages, using page-specific parameters so users can adjust business thresholds themselves without repeated analyst redevelopment. KPI cards, measures, dynamic headings and contextual interpretation text all update immediately when a threshold changes, so the report explains the business meaning of the selected settings as well as the resulting numbers. Automated exception flagging with conditional colouring and exception-only scatter plots replaced a more manual process for locating outliers, making the cases that need investigation materially easier to find.

I then rebuilt and enhanced a Power App embedded within Power BI to support the investigation workflow itself: case statuses, categorisation and bulk commenting. Where multiple selected cases share the same outcome, bulk updates reduce repetitive update actions by up to approximately 95 per cent, and the investigation results feed back into governed enterprise reporting. Power Automate flows ingest secure Excel input into a SharePoint List and remove or reset SharePoint records, and the operational list is then ingested back into Fabric using Dataflow Gen2 to support a closed-loop analytics workflow.

The solution connected exception reporting directly to the investigation workflow, allowing users to identify, categorise and update cases within the same governed process.

Marketing: customer lifetime value, churn and retention

Marketing needed to understand the relationship between retention and customer value, but any single definition of churn embeds an assumption. A patient who has not returned in twelve months means something different from one who has not returned in thirty-six, and the right threshold is a business judgement rather than a data one.

I built end-to-end marketing analytics covering customer demographics, retention, churn, Customer Lifetime Value and basket analysis. Rather than hard-coding one definition, I developed a flexible churn framework with 12, 24 and 36-month churn windows and 3, 6, 9 and 12-month patient cohorts, and designed the analytical logic so a change in churn assumption flows consistently through the related customer-value measures instead of requiring separate report versions.

The flexible churn framework allowed Marketing to test alternative assumptions and immediately see how they affected retention and customer value, instead of working to one fixed definition of what a customer is worth.

Finance: governed self-service reporting

Finance needed governed enterprise numbers but also needed to keep working in Excel, where their own calculations and PivotTable analysis live. Those two requirements usually pull against each other, and the gap was being filled with manual lookups across fragmented sources.

I built two Excel-based Finance reports connected to the Fabric SQL analytics endpoint, supported by automated daily-refresh pipelines, consolidating multiple financial dimensions and measures into governed, daily-refreshed outputs. Finance keeps the flexibility of Excel for independent calculations and PivotTable analysis, but the underlying numbers are now governed enterprise data rather than assembled by hand. I also built reporting for taxation purposes and additional finance and variance analysis, with reconciliation and sign-off before production release.

People & Culture: point-in-time workforce analytics

Workforce reporting relied on static month-end reporting, which answers what the headcount is now but not what it was at an arbitrary past date. I redesigned it using historical snapshots in Fabric so headcount and turnover can be analysed at any historical point in time, and created curated fact and dimension structures with reusable reporting logic to improve workforce metric consistency and historical analysis.

Enterprise data governance: governed master location mapping

Location definitions and mapping were inconsistent, which quietly undermines any State or Country level reporting built on top of them. I created and validated a governed practice-region-country mapping across Australia and New Zealand, validated it across both markets, and it was adopted as the governed master location mapping and used to derive consistent State and Country attributes for patient and downstream reporting.

This is the clearest example of my work extending past report design into enterprise data governance: a reference asset other people's reporting now depends on.

Reconciliation, tie-outs & audit

Validated outputs across three layers, source extracts, curated datasets and final report outputs, using reconciliation tie-outs and audit checks at each boundary. Built an exception report to surface missing reference mappings impacting downstream transaction reporting (for example, unmapped payment attributes or missing staff identifiers), which improved completeness and reduced remediation time when source data drifted.

Reporting & delivery

Delivered reporting across all four domains in Power BI and Excel, covering KPI and variance reporting, point-in-time snapshots and exception monitoring, drawing on Dynamics 365, PayGlobal and multiple practice-management-system structures. Delivery ran through SME discovery, modelling, unit testing, reconciliation, UAT, issue resolution, documentation, stakeholder approval and controlled production deployment, with progress tracking and stakeholder updates through Azure DevOps and Confluence.

Outcomes

Finance, Marketing, Clinical Assurance and People & Culture now consume governed metrics from a shared Direct Lake semantic model with reusable measures, giving a clear audit path from source through curated layer to published figure, with Australia and New Zealand on shared solutions rather than duplicated logic.

What changed for each group is specific. Clinical Assurance users adjust their own thresholds and categorise cases in bulk instead of queuing report changes with an analyst. Marketing can test alternative churn assumptions and see the effect on retention and customer value immediately. Finance works in Excel against governed daily-refreshed enterprise data instead of manual lookups. People & Culture can report headcount and turnover at any historical point rather than only the latest month-end. State and Country reporting resolves consistently through a governed governed master location mapping.

Reflection

The most valuable parts of this work were not the individual visuals. They were the curation logic, the reusable governed measures and the reconciliation tie-outs that made the numbers trustworthy, and the decision in Clinical Assurance to change the workflow rather than just the report. Building a dashboard that answers a question is straightforward. Building one that lets a business user answer their own next question, and the one after that, is what actually removes the analyst bottleneck.
