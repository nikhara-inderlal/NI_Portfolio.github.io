Enterprise Healthcare Reporting Rebuild

Rebuilding Finance & Assurance reporting on Microsoft Fabric with a shared Direct Lake semantic model, T-SQL and Spark notebook curation, and point-in-time workforce reporting.

SectorHealthcare
RoleAnalytics Engineer / Power BI Developer
EngagementSep 2025 – Present
ToolsFabric (Lakehouse/Warehouse, Direct Lake), Power BI, T-SQL, Spark, Azure DevOps, Confluence

Context

Finance & Assurance reporting across the client was fragmented: different teams produced the same metric with different numbers, there was no shared dimensional model, and refresh and validation were largely manual. Without a common semantic layer it was difficult to reconcile KPIs or to give finance leadership a defensible audit path from source to published figure.

Business problem

Stakeholders needed a single, governed reporting layer that could support KPI and variance reporting, point-in-time snapshots and exception monitoring — while also reducing remediation time when source data had gaps. The previous setup didn’t expose missing reference mappings, so downstream transaction reporting silently lost rows.

Approach

Contributed to rebuilding and standardising Finance & Assurance reporting using a shared semantic model on Direct Lake, with curated fact and dimension structures to improve consistency and scalability. The work spanned the curation layer (T-SQL and Spark notebooks in Fabric), the semantic model (reusable base measures), the report layer (Power BI and Excel) and the delivery layer (UAT, controlled DEV–TEST deployments).

Modelling & preparation

Created and enriched reporting tables using T-SQL and Spark notebooks in Fabric Lakehouse and Warehouse. Built reusable base measures so the same KPI meant the same thing across reports. Extended the dimensional model to support point-in-time reporting for workforce analysis.

Headcount and Turnover reporting was a specific build: ingested historical workforce snapshot data into Fabric, implemented snapshot logic in the Fabric Warehouse, and extended the dimensional model so headcount and turnover could be reported at any historical point in time rather than only at the latest state.

Reconciliation, tie-outs & audit

Validated outputs across three layers — source extracts, curated datasets and final report outputs — using reconciliation/tie-outs and audit checks at each boundary. Built an exception report to surface missing reference mappings impacting downstream transaction reporting (for example, unmapped payment attributes or missing staff identifiers), which improved completeness and reduced remediation time when source data drifted.

Reporting & delivery

Delivered a suite of Finance & Assurance reporting outputs in Power BI and Excel, covering KPI and variance reporting, point-in-time snapshots and exception monitoring. Worked with D365 Finance data where the reporting drew on financial source systems. Supported UAT and controlled DEV–TEST deployments with progress tracking and stakeholder updates through Azure DevOps and Confluence.

Outcomes

Finance & Assurance now consume governed metrics from a shared Direct Lake semantic model, with a clear audit path from source through curated layer to published figure. Point-in-time workforce reporting is supported through the snapshot extension to the dimensional model, and the exception report surfaces reference-mapping gaps before they reach downstream transaction reports.

Reflection

The most valuable parts of this work weren’t the individual visuals — they were the curation logic, the reusable measures and the reconciliation/tie-outs that made the numbers trustworthy. Treating data quality as a first-class deliverable (unit tests, tie-outs, audit logging, exception reporting) is what made the rebuild stick.
