Enterprise Healthcare Reporting Rebuild

Healthcare reporting rebuild across Finance, Assurance and HR or P&C domains using Microsoft Fabric, Power BI, T-SQL, Spark notebooks, Direct Lake semantic modelling and reconciliation checks.

SectorHealthcare
RoleAnalytics Engineer / Power BI Developer / Fabric Developer
EngagementSep 2025 to Present
ToolsFabric (Lakehouse/Warehouse, Direct Lake), Dataflow Gen2, Power BI, T-SQL, Spark, D365 Finance, SharePoint Lists, Power Apps, Azure DevOps, Confluence

Context

A healthcare organisation needed to rebuild reporting across Finance, Assurance and HR or P&C domains. Reporting was fragmented across teams and domains, with inconsistent metric definitions, limited shared modelling and manual validation work, and gaps between source systems, curated reporting tables and final Power BI outputs. Without a common semantic layer it was difficult to reconcile KPIs or to give leadership a defensible audit path from source to published figure.

Business problem

Stakeholders needed a single, governed reporting layer that could support KPI and variance reporting, point-in-time snapshots and exception monitoring, while also reducing remediation time when source data had gaps. The previous setup did not expose missing reference mappings, so downstream transaction reporting silently lost rows.

Approach

Contributed to rebuilding and standardising reporting across Finance, Assurance and HR or P&C domains using a shared semantic model on Direct Lake, with curated fact and dimension structures to improve consistency and scalability. The work spanned the curation layer (T-SQL and Spark notebooks in Fabric), the semantic model layer (reusable base measures), the report layer (Power BI and Excel), and a user workflow layer where SharePoint List data was brought into the reporting workflow, transformed into reporting-ready structures and connected into Power BI. Delivery used UAT and controlled DEV to TEST deployments.

Modelling & preparation

Created and enriched reporting tables using T-SQL and Spark notebooks in Fabric Lakehouse and Warehouse. Built reusable base measures so the same KPI meant the same thing across reports. Extended the dimensional model to support point-in-time reporting for workforce analysis.

Headcount and Turnover reporting was a specific build: ingested historical workforce snapshot data into Fabric, implemented snapshot logic in the Fabric Warehouse, and extended the dimensional model so headcount and turnover could be reported at any historical point in time rather than only at the latest state.

Reconciliation, tie-outs & audit

Validated outputs across three layers, source extracts, curated datasets and final report outputs, using reconciliation tie-outs and audit checks at each boundary. Built an exception report to surface missing reference mappings impacting downstream transaction reporting (for example, unmapped payment attributes or missing staff identifiers), which improved completeness and reduced remediation time when source data drifted.

SharePoint Lists, Power Apps and Power BI integration

Integrated SharePoint Lists, Microsoft Fabric, Power BI and Power Apps into an interactive reporting workflow. SharePoint List data was ingested into Fabric, transformed into reporting-ready structures and surfaced in Power BI. A Power Apps app was embedded inside the Power BI report so users could update or edit list-based operational data while viewing relevant reporting context. This helped move the report from a static reporting layer toward a more interactive business workflow.

Reporting & delivery

Delivered a suite of reporting outputs across Finance, Assurance and HR or P&C domains in Power BI and Excel, covering KPI and variance reporting, point-in-time snapshots and exception monitoring. Worked with D365 Finance data where the reporting drew on financial source systems. Supported UAT and controlled DEV to TEST deployments with progress tracking and stakeholder updates through Azure DevOps and Confluence.

Outcomes

Finance, Assurance and HR or P&C teams now consume governed metrics from a shared Direct Lake semantic model with reusable measures, giving a clear audit path from source through curated layer to published figure. Point-in-time workforce reporting is supported through the snapshot extension to the dimensional model, exception reporting surfaces reference-mapping gaps before they reach downstream transaction reports, and SharePoint Lists with embedded Power Apps let users update operational data in reporting context. Together these improved trust in reporting outputs across the three domains.

Reflection

The most valuable parts of this work were not the individual visuals; they were the curation logic, the reusable measures and the reconciliation tie-outs that made the numbers trustworthy. Treating data quality as a first-class deliverable (unit tests, tie-outs, audit logging, exception reporting) is what made the rebuild stick.
