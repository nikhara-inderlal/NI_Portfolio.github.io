Fleet Fuel Efficiency Analytics at Teletrac Navman

End-to-end telematics data engineering across a 170+ vehicle fleet, and a fuel-efficiency solution I proposed and built outside the scope I was given.

SectorTeletrac Navman, a leading ANZ fleet-management provider
RoleAnalytics Consultant / Data Engineer / Tableau Developer
Engagement170+ vehicle fleet
ToolsAlteryx, Tableau, Spatial analytics, API integration, Fuel pricing data, Excel

Context

Teletrac Navman is a leading fleet-management provider in Australia and New Zealand. The engagement covered a fleet of more than 170 vehicles, where TN360 was capturing vehicle, event and user data through its APIs. The raw records described what happened: trips, distances, engine events. They said very little about what any of it cost.

Business problem

The telematics feed lacked the context needed for decisions. There were no reliable vehicle reference details, no consumption benchmark to compare actual use against, and no pricing to convert litres into dollars. Without that enrichment the data could not answer the practical questions: which vehicles, routes or drivers are burning more fuel than they should, how much is being wasted, and what is that worth as fuel prices move.

Approach

I was engaged for the data engineering, and that came first: an end-to-end Alteryx ETL pipeline integrating TN360 vehicle, event and user API data with spreadsheet and external reference data to produce clean, analysis-ready telematics datasets. Incomplete vehicle records were a blocker for any benchmarking, so I enriched them using VIN and registration information to recover the make, model and year attributes that fuel-consumption benchmarking depends on.

While building that pipeline it became clear the enriched data could answer a question nobody had asked me to answer. I identified the analytical opportunity outside my assigned data-engineering scope, proposed the solution, and built a fully functional Tableau fuel-efficiency dashboard end to end in approximately one day.

Data enrichment & preparation

The Alteryx workflow joined telematics records to vehicle reference data for make, model and expected consumption, applied fuel-consumption benchmarks so actual use could be measured against expectation, and integrated historical and current Australian fuel-pricing data so litres translated into cost at trip level. Spreadsheet-based enrichment closed the remaining gaps in the reference data before the final dataset was produced.

Fuel pricing & API integration

Australian fuel pricing was integrated and aligned to the relevant periods, so cost reflected real price movement rather than a flat assumption. Keeping pricing as a managed input meant the same workflow stayed accurate as prices changed, without reworking the model each time.

The analytical step that made the difference was developing actual-versus-optimised travel and idling logic. Comparing what a trip did cost against what it should have cost is what converts a fuel figure into an actionable one: it quantifies fuel and monetary loss from inefficient routes, excessive idling and vehicle or driver behaviour, including Saved and Wasted classifications.

Reporting

The Tableau dashboard covered total and average fuel cost and usage, monthly actual versus optimised trends, vehicle and driver consumption categories, idle-loss analysis and route-efficiency cost comparisons. Vehicle and driver classifications made the comparison fair, so a heavy vehicle on long routes was not flagged simply for consuming more than a light one.

Outcomes

Operations moved from raw telematics logs to a clear view of where fuel was being wasted and what it was costing, with inefficient routes, heavy-usage vehicles and excessive idling visible and comparable rather than inferred.

I presented the client-ready solution directly to stakeholders without the interim review cycle the other team solutions went through. It received strong final client feedback and was selected as a joint winner of the internal dashboard challenge.

Reflection

The engineering was the assignment; the efficiency analysis was the opportunity. The reason I could see it was that I had been inside the data long enough to know what it could support, which is an argument for analytics engineers staying close to the business question rather than stopping at a clean dataset. Keeping fuel pricing as a managed input was the other decision that mattered, because it meant the solution stayed useful after handover instead of aging out with the price data.
