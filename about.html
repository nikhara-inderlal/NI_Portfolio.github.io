About

A quantitative foundation behind practical analytics delivery.

My background in Mathematics and Statistical Modelling is not valuable because I studied mathematics. It is valuable because most reporting arguments are not technical arguments. They are disagreements about what a metric means, whether a comparison is fair, or whether a number can be defended. That is the part I am trained for, and it is what I bring to a delivery team alongside the build.

Foundation

Mathematics & statistical modelling

My BSc in Mathematics, with a major in Mathematics and a minor in Statistical Modelling, included applied statistics, probability, linear models and applied statistical methods. I then spent two years teaching Business Statistics at Macquarie University, which is where I learned to explain a statistical assumption to someone who does not want a statistics lecture. Both halves of that turn out to matter in consulting.

In practice it shows up in concrete places. At an independent school, measuring student improvement fairly was a metric-design problem, not a Power BI problem: raw averages punished students who were already at the top, and peer comparison ignored that year-level difficulty rises over time. On the healthcare engagement I found patient counts that disagreed between reports because legacy logic mixed appointment-based activity counts with patient-dimension cohort counts. Neither of those is found by building faster. They are found by asking what the number is actually counting.

Analytics engineering

Where rigour meets delivery

The rigour only counts if something ships. I work end to end on my engagements: governed semantic models, curated fact and dimension structures, reusable base measures and audit-logged pipelines in Microsoft Fabric and BigQuery, then the reporting layer on top and the validation that makes it defensible.

The part I find most interesting is the join between the two. A stakeholder describes a problem in business language, the data describes something slightly different, and the work is reconciling those two things honestly rather than building whatever was literally asked for. That is also why I would rather run the requirements session myself than receive a specification.

Working style

How I approach analytics projects

On my current engagement I run each of these steps myself, from the first SME session through to controlled deployment into production.

Understand the question

I lead the requirements sessions with subject-matter experts directly, clarifying the business purpose, the audience, the decision it feeds and what a useful output would actually look like.

Inspect the data

I review source structure, quality issues, grain, relationships and existing logic before committing to an approach, because the honest answer is usually constrained by what the data can support.

Model with intent

I build to strict star-schema structures with reusable governed measures, so the same KPI means the same thing everywhere instead of being redefined report by report.

Validate the numbers

I reconcile across source, curated and report layers, unit test the logic, investigate anomalies to a cause and document the limitations before anything reaches a stakeholder.

Design for users

I design around how people investigate a problem, not around a metric list. Where it helps, that means self-service thresholds and embedded workflow so users can act without raising a change request.

Deliver and hand over

I walk stakeholders through the result, incorporate their feedback, produce the technical and business documentation, secure sign-off and deploy through TEST to PROD.
